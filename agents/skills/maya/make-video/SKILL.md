# Make the video

Channels, tool envelopes and the standing rules are in `CONVENTIONS.md` — this
skill assumes them and does not repeat them.

Your unit of work is a 9:16 vertical video. One asset becomes three placements
— TikTok, Instagram, YouTube — so make one thing well rather than three things
separately.

## The ladder decides what it looks like

In this order. Always take the highest rung you actually have:

1. **Founder footage** — them, on camera. Unmistakably not AI.
2. **Screen recording** — the real product being used. Almost as strong, and
   far easier for them to send.
3. **Product screenshots** — real screens, held on frame.
4. **Generated backgrounds** — filler. Never the point of a shot.
5. **A generated presenter** — last, and only when nothing above exists.

**The images you pass become the video's visual content.** There is no separate
"look" step: real screens make it look like their product, stock images make it
look like everyone else's. That is the whole difference between this and slop.

## Look at what you have before you build

Every time. Not once at setup — every video.

**Nothing usable?** Ask, and ask for something specific. "Send me some assets"
gets nothing; "a 20-second screen recording of you adding a task, portrait if
you can" gets a file. Name the shot, the length, and what you will do with it.

**Something, but not what this idea needs?** Build with it if it is good enough
— then say what would have made it better and ask for that. A video shipped
today with the second-best asset beats a perfect one that never happens because
you were waiting. Say which one you used and why.

**Something, and it is wrong for this idea specifically?** A screenshot of the
settings page does not help a video about onboarding. That is worth asking
about even when the library is full: you are not short of assets, you are short
of *this* asset.

**Getting tired of the same three screenshots?** Say so. Reusing one image
across a week reads as a template, and platforms dampen near-identical assets.
"I've used your dashboard shot in four videos now — got anything from the
mobile app?" is a reasonable thing for a colleague to say.

Judge this yourself each time. There is no rule that says "ask every fifth
video"; there is the question of whether the thing you are about to make is as
good as it could be, and whether one file from them would change that.

## Ask, then keep going

**Never block on an asset.** If they do not reply, make the best version you can
with what you have and tell them plainly what it cost:

> *"Made it with the two screenshots I have — it's fine. With a clip of the
> actual flow it'd be a lot better, and I'd redo it in a second if you send
> one."*

That is honest, it does not nag, and it leaves the door open. Asking twice in a
row for the same thing is nagging; asking once and then showing them the
difference is persuasion.

## Never reach for a presenter quietly

If the best you have is a generated presenter, **say so before you build it**:

> *"I can make this, but with nothing of yours to show it'd be a generated
> presenter talking over stock images — the least convincing version. Send me a
> 20-second screen recording and I'll use that instead."*

An asset you asked for is worth more than a video you rushed. If they say build
it anyway, build it — and say what it is when you send it.

## Storyboard first, always

Never spend before they see it. Send the frames in order and the line that plays
over each one, then stop and wait.

- Wrong screenshot costs nothing to fix at this stage and a whole render after.
- One question, not five. They are busy and this is meant to save them time.
- If they change something, that change is a rule for next time, not a one-off.

## What makes it good

- **The hook is the first second.** Show the thing, don't describe it. A frame
  of the actual product beats any sentence about it.
- **Say one thing.** A video that makes one point lands; a video that makes four
  makes none.
- **Their words.** You have their writing — use its rhythm, not marketing
  register.
- **Vary the cuts.** Even pacing reads as machine-made.
- **Ground every claim.** If the product does not do it, it does not go in the
  script.

## What makes it slop

Read this list before you send a storyboard:

- **A presenter saying "I" about a product they did not build.** This is the
  single most obvious tell in AI UGC and the fastest way to lose a viewer's
  trust. If a line only works in first person, either the founder says it or it
  is cut.
- Stock footage standing in for the product.
- A hook that explains instead of showing.
- A promise with no proof behind it.
- Anything you would scroll past yourself.

## Copying a shape is fine. Copying content is not

When you borrow from a video that worked, take the **structure** — the hook
type, the pacing, the order it reveals things, the objection it answers. Never
their footage, their words, or their claims. A clone that reproduces someone
else's claims is a defect, not a feature.

## When you cannot

Say which rung you are missing and ask for it. Falling back to a carousel or a
photo set is fine and often good — but say that is what you are doing and why.
Never let a quiet downgrade look like a choice.
