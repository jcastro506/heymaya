# Watch content

Rules and channels are in `CONVENTIONS.md`.

Watching a competitor's ad and watching an organic reel are the same job: you
are looking at video in this niche and asking what makes it land. The only
difference is how much someone else has already risked on it, and that is a
ranking rule, not a separate activity.

## Rank by what it cost someone

**An ad that has been running for weeks is the strongest signal available.**
Companies kill losing ads fast, so an old ad is a tested one. What matters is
**how long it has run and how many variants exist** — not that an ad exists at
all. Anyone can boost a post for twenty dollars, and that looks identical in a
library.

Then organic posts beating their own account's baseline. Then complaints. Then
trends, which are presence rather than proof.

## What to actually take

**The structure, never the content.** What the hook is. What it promises. Which
objection it answers before the viewer raises it. How fast it cuts, what it
shows first, what it withholds. That travels between products.

**Never their footage, their words, or their claims.** A video that reproduces
someone else's claims about their own product is a defect, and one that reuses
their footage is worse than a defect.

## Read the offer, not just the shape

The most useful thing in a competitor's ad is usually the **promise** — what
they think their buyer wants badly enough to pay to say it. That informs a
script far more than the edit does.

## Paid does not mean it will travel organically

An ad buys its impressions; an organic post has to earn them. Something that
converts cold traffic can still be throttled organically for reading as an
advert. When you borrow from an ad, re-shape it native: hook in the first
second, no advert tells, pacing that belongs on the platform.

## Be honest when it is thin

Some weeks the niche is quiet and some sweeps come back with nothing usable.
Say so. **"Nothing worth copying this week"** is a real finding and the
founder can act on it. Filling a week with weak material because a slot exists
is how a content calendar becomes noise.

If a channel returns nothing repeatedly, that is worth flagging as a problem
rather than reporting as a quiet niche — they are different things.
