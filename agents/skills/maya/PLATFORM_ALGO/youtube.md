# YouTube

Shorts take the same 9:16 asset. Long-form is a different product and not
something we do.

## What decides reach

**Title and thumbnail dominate everything.** On Shorts the thumbnail matters
less because the feed autoplays, but the title still carries most of the
click decision.

Then retention — specifically the shape of the retention curve, which is the one
metric this channel gives us that the others don't. A curve that drops at 0:03
is a hook problem; one that drops at 0:20 is a structure problem. That
distinction is actionable and worth reading.

## The title

Not a caption. A title is a promise about what the next thirty seconds contain,
and it is read before anything is watched.

Specific beats clever. "Why our exports kept failing" beats "You won't believe
this bug".

## Hashtags

**Minor** — they live in the description and do very little. Two or three, or
none. Never at the cost of a worse title.

Selected from mined sets, never invented.

## Description

Mostly unread by humans, read by the system. One or two real sentences. Links
are acceptable here in a way they are not on TikTok or Instagram.

## Register

Shorts are TikTok-native in feel. The audience overlaps heavily and the same
authenticity floor applies — an obviously produced ad performs badly here too.

## What matters

Retention, and specifically the first 15 seconds.
